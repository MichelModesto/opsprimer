If you're shopping for marketing automation and you start with HubSpot's free CRM
because everyone recommends it, there's something the recommendation usually leaves
out.

On the free plan, HubSpot allows **one automated action**. One automated email per
form. That's the whole automation surface.

And workflows — the thing people actually mean when they say marketing automation —
aren't in the entry paid tier either. They're a Professional feature.

## The number that reframes the question

From HubSpot's own product catalog:

| Tier | What you can automate |
|---|---|
| Free | **1 automated email per form** · **1 automated action** |
| Starter | Still no workflows |
| Professional | Unlimited actions, omni-channel workflows |

So "start on HubSpot free and upgrade when you need automation" isn't a small step
up. The step is to Professional, and Professional is a different order of spending
from the $7-a-seat Starter that gets quoted as the entry price.

None of that makes HubSpot a bad CRM. It's a very good free CRM. It just means that
if **automation** is the reason you're shopping, you're evaluating it on the one axis
where its free tier has almost nothing to give you.

## What an agency actually wants to automate

Before comparing tools, it's worth being specific, because "marketing automation"
covers at least four different jobs and the tools are good at different ones.

**Lead follow-up.** Someone fills a form; they get a sequence, not a single email.
This is the one most agencies mean, and it's the one HubSpot free explicitly caps at
one action.

**Internal routing.** A deal moves stage, a task appears for the right person, a
Slack message fires. This is closer to operations than marketing, and it's usually
where the "no-code" promise breaks first.

**Client reporting.** Data leaves the CRM on a schedule and lands somewhere a client
can read. Almost always ends up needing something outside the CRM.

**Onboarding.** Contract signed, and a chain of setup steps runs. Multi-system by
definition, so it rarely lives inside one tool.

Only the first is really "marketing automation" as sold. The other three are why
agencies end up with a stack instead of a product.

## Where each option actually lands

**HubSpot** is a CRM that happens to include marketing tools. I set one up and ran
it: the database, the pipeline and the record-keeping are genuinely good and
genuinely free. The automation is not there until Professional.

**ActiveCampaign** is the inverse — it was built as automation with a CRM attached.
That's the right shape if sequences are the point. Two things to check before you
commit: there is **no free tier**, only a 14-day trial, and on the entry Starter tier
**deal pipelines aren't included** — they're a paid CRM add-on. So the tool that's
better at automation is worse at the thing article one was about.

That trade is the actual decision. Not a feature grid.

## The honest recommendation

- **You want a CRM and can live without automation for now:** HubSpot free. It's the
  best free CRM available and you lose nothing by starting there — as long as you know
  automation means Professional later, not Starter.
- **Automated sequences are the whole reason you're shopping:** ActiveCampaign, and
  price the tier that includes what you need rather than the entry one.
- **You need both, properly, on a small budget:** you're going to be unhappy with
  either. This is the case where an agency ends up with a cheap CRM plus a separate
  email tool, wired together — which works, and is more common than vendors admit.

## What I'd check before paying anyone

Write down the three automations you'd build in week one. Not ten — three. Then open
the pricing page of whichever tool you're considering and find the tier that includes
all three. That tier is your real price.

Most people price the entry tier, build nothing, and discover the gap in month two.
The gap between advertised entry price and the tier that does the job is the single
most reliable surprise in this category.
